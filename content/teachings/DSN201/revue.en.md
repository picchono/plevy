## The literature review
The research question is a pivotal element in the development of a research project, **between an observation of the study context and a directed analysis** that will attempt to make a proposal of original knowledge. It is often the logical follow-up to the elucidation of the study context (we find ourselves in such a situation, with such stakes and unknowns, so our research question is such). It precedes the statement of the research conduct (which of course relies on the research question).

The research question is therefore the driving force behind the research conduct, and justifies the resources engaged in the research and its direction. Coupled with the research hypothesis, which normally follows it, **it is the foundation of the research** and of the scientific article.

## The constituent elements of a literature review
A literature review consists of different elements that allow elaborating a topic that will result in a state of the art.

### Exploring and organising the literature on the studied topic
The description of the topic is the subject of a preliminary exercise to try to circumscribe the topic. It involves explaining the topic, its position in the research world (whether it is in line with or breaks from identified work, its eco-social or industrial relevance, whether it targets a new field, a contribution or a debate in an identified field...).

Priority is given to referenced articles and the most recent writings on the studied topic.

### Analysing the selected sources
Tools that allow analysis and classification of articles must be used.

This involves:
- identifying the arguments developed by each source and the conditions under which these arguments were elaborated,
- discussing the limits and possible or established contradictions to these arguments,
- pointing out research questions that may emerge.

### Organising and discussing the results of the analysis
The state of the art should include:
- an introduction presenting the object and content,
- an explanation of the topic and its stakes,
- a review showing the links, articulations, and tensions between the different elements collected and selected for the literature review,
- a proposal opening on one or more problematics related to the topic,
- a bibliography bringing together all the works addressed (and sometimes other series of works found but not exploited).
